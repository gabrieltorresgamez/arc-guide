## Explanation

A business process management suite (BPMS) is software for modeling, running, and managing business processes. Organizations buy it for different reasons. A report on a Gartner talk groups these reasons into four main buying patterns, also called usage scenarios:

1. **Automating a specific process.** The organization automates one specific process.
2. **Continuous process improvement.** The organization keeps refining its processes over time.
3. **Moving to SOA.** The organization redesigns around a process-based, service-oriented architecture. The article Service-Oriented Architecture covers the basics.
4. **Business transformation.** The BPMS supports a business transformation initiative.

Each scenario has its own kind of buyer and its own goal. These differences can mean quite different requirements for a BPMS, depending on what drives the original purchase.

## Example

A regional insurer wants to speed up its claims handling. It first treats this as automating a specific process, its own claims process. Later it decides to offer claims steps as shared services to other applications. That is a move toward a process-based SOA. The goals change with each step.

## Trade-offs

The scenarios classify buying reasons. They are not a method. Use the list as a prompt for discussion, not as a rulebook.

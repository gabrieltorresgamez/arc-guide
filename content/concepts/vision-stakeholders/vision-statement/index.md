## Explanation

A vision statement gives everyone involved in development one shared goal. It should be short and inspiring. Pichler advises a brief statement or slogan that is big and inspiring.

It is a concise summary of the idea. It describes the intention and motivation behind the product. Scrum encourages a vision shared by the product owner and the team.

**What the Vision Board covers besides the vision**

- **Target customers.** Name the market or segment you address. Choose a homogeneous, clear-cut group, especially for a new product.
- **Customer need and value.** Make clear why people will want the product or be willing to pay for it.
- **Differentiation.** Name the few features that make the product stand out. Listing lots of features is a mistake. One source suggests three to five standout features.

**Why it helps architecture work**

The architecture documentation starts with a short overview of the requirements and driving forces (see arc42 Introduction and Goals). It also asks you to know all stakeholders involved or affected. Stakeholders decide how much architectural work is needed and how detailed it is. See the articles Stakeholder and Stakeholder Concern.

## Example

An invented vision for a small meal-planning app:

"For busy parents who struggle to plan weekly dinners, our app suggests a full week of meals in one tap and builds the shopping list."

The statement names one customer group and one need. It shows a clear benefit and mentions only two standout features. It fits in two sentences, so a team can remember it.

## Trade-offs

Keeping the statement short means leaving things out. The sources advise against listing lots of features, and they favor a clear-cut target group, especially for a new product.

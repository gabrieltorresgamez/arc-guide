## Explanation

ATAM is the Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method, from the Software Engineering Institute. It evaluates an architecture against quality goals. See the article "Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM)" for the full picture.

An evaluation usually takes three to four days in total. It brings together a trained evaluation team, the architects and representatives of the architecture's stakeholders.

The method runs in two phases. Phase 1 consists of steps 1 to 6. It starts with step 1: the evaluation leader describes the evaluation method to everyone present.

Phase 2 covers steps 7 to 9 and finishes the evaluation. It widens the input to the larger stakeholder group. That group brainstorms scenarios, the results are re-analyzed, and the results are presented.

The purpose of the whole method is to help choose a suitable architecture. It does this by finding trade-offs and sensitivity points. Trade-offs show how quality goals interact and trade off against each other. See "Trade-off Point" and "Sensitivity Point".

## Example

A team plans to evaluate the architecture of an order system. At the start, the evaluation leader meets the evaluation team and the architects. The leader explains how the evaluation will proceed. Only after this shared start does the work move on. Later, a wider stakeholder group joins to finish the evaluation.

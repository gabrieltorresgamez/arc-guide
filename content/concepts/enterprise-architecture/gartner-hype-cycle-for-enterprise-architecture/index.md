## Explanation

A hype cycle is a chart of how expectations around a topic change over time. Gartner publishes one for enterprise architecture (EA). EA aligns business and IT structures; see the article "Enterprise Architecture".

**Phases**

- **Technology Trigger:** a breakthrough or product launch creates press and interest.
- **Peak of Inflated Expectations:** publicity creates over-enthusiasm and unrealistic expectations.
- **Trough of Disillusionment:** technologies fail to meet expectations and become unfashionable.
- **Plateau of Productivity:** the stage where topics are in productive use. Below, it appears through examples such as EA tools in 2024.

**What editions showed**

- **2010:** EA itself slipped into the trough, along with EA tools. Gartner did not expect it to stay there.
- **2024:** EA tools had been missing a year earlier. They returned and reached the plateau, according to a vendor summary.
- **2025:** AI agents, orchestration frameworks and AI governance crowded the peak. Platform engineering, composite apps and data and analytics architecture were listed as nearing the plateau.

The pattern is that topics move. A topic can sit in the trough and later recover.

**Sensible use**

A hype cycle can help an EA team judge whether it is the right time to invest in a new technology. One source argues that swift followers beat bleeding-edge adopters.

**Criticism**

One critic called the 2025 report an "AI bingo card". They said it gives mainstream and embryonic practices the same emphasis, which muddles prioritization. They listed business architecture, communities of practice and roadmapping as mature practices.

## Example

An architecture team sees a new topic near the peak in the latest chart. They do not start a large rollout. They run a small trial and watch the topic's position in later editions. They invest more once it nears the plateau. They also check the chart's emphasis against their own priorities.

## Trade-offs

The chart has limits. One critic says it gives mature and embryonic practices the same emphasis, which muddles prioritization. Treat it as one input next to your own needs.

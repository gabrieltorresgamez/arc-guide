## Explanation

See the article Enterprise Architecture. A maturity level describes how developed the EA department is.

**Value is the measure.** Info-Tech treats the value the organization actually gains as the true sign of maturity. Measuring operational maturity can only go so far.

**The target should fit the organization.** Info-Tech says the target should be right-sized to the organization's size and complexity. The top level is not necessarily right for every EA department.

**Assess, set a target, plan.** The Info-Tech maturity blueprint works in three parts:

1. Assess the current maturity of the EA department.
2. Define the optimal target for the organization.
3. Produce a roadmap of EA initiatives.

Info-Tech's assessment brief says its auto-generated goals and roadmap turn improvement into a step-by-step process.

**Other models exist.** TOGAF (see the article TOGAF) includes the Architecture Capability Maturity Model (ACMM). The US Department of Commerce developed it. It nominally has six levels, numbered 0 to 5. The NASCIO model is another option. It was designed for state governments.

## Example

A small company has a three-person EA team. Leadership first assesses where the team stands today. It then picks a target that matches the company's size and complexity, instead of copying a larger company's level. Finally, it lists the initiatives that would move the team toward that target, and it checks progress by the value the business gains.

## Trade-offs

Aiming for the top level is not always sensible. The right target depends on the organization's size and complexity, and the top level is not necessarily appropriate for every EA department.

Choosing a model also has limits. The ACMM is described as best suited to organizations that already have EA experience. The NASCIO model is not suitable for non-governmental organizations.

## Explanation

TOGAF is a framework for architecture work across a whole organization. Development began in 1995. It drew mainly on TAFIM, a US Department of Defense framework for information management.

**The core: the ADM.** The Architecture Development Method (ADM) is the core of the framework.

**Four domains.** TOGAF is based on four architecture domains: Business, Application, Data and Technology. Related articles cover Business Architecture, Application Architecture, Data Architecture and Technology Architecture.

**Content framework.** TOGAF 9 added a formal content metamodel that links TOGAF's artifacts together.

**Enterprise Continuum.** This is a classification structure for building blocks and artifacts. It has two parts: the Architecture Continuum and the Solutions Continuum.

**Current edition.** The 10th Edition is split into the TOGAF Fundamental Content and the TOGAF Series Guides.

**More about TOGAF.**

- **TOGAF Architecture Development Method:** The TOGAF Architecture Development Method (ADM) is a cyclic, repeatable method for developing an enterprise architecture. An organization can tailor it, move between phases, and adapt it to agile work with sprints.
- **TOGAF Phase C: Application Architecture:** Application Architecture in TOGAF Phase C designs the target applications that support the business architecture and vision. It compares today's applications with the target and turns the gaps into roadmap components.
- **Data Architecture Inputs:** Data architecture inputs are the materials TOGAF's Phase C gathers before designing the target data architecture.

## Example

A company wants to improve its order handling. The architects use the ADM as their working cycle. They describe the business, data, application and technology domains. They pick the artifacts they need from the content framework. They file reusable pieces in the Enterprise Continuum classification.
